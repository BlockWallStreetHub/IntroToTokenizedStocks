Module 1

How We Got Here

Tokenized equities didn't start on Robinhood Chain, and understanding the path here matters — it tells you why the current moment is different from the last two waves, and why liquidity is concentrating where it is right now instead of somewhere else.

Era One: Ondo Finance Sets The Standard

For a long stretch, if you wanted credibly-backed tokenized stock exposure, Ondo Stocks (originally launched as Ondo Global Markets) was the default answer. It became the reference point for what a "real" tokenized stock should look like, because every token is fully backed by actual shares and ETFs held with U.S. broker-dealers or U.S.-chartered trust companies — not a synthetic promise with nothing behind it.

Its signature innovation is what Ondo calls just-in-time liquidity. Instead of relying on a thin, pre-funded pool of tokens (the model many early competitors used, where a big trade could move the price sharply against you), Ondo's minting and redemption process taps directly into real Nasdaq and NYSE liquidity at the moment of the trade. The effect on execution cost was dramatic: some competing platforms showed roughly 200 basis points of price impact on a $200,000 tokenized SPY trade at launch. Ondo Stocks reduced that to around 2 basis points — a hundredfold improvement — by connecting straight to liquidity that already existed rather than trying to bootstrap a new pool from scratch.

On top of the execution model, Ondo built in institutional-grade protections that most early tokenized-stock platforms skipped: an independent third-party Verification Agent reviews the asset backing and publishes a report every business day, and an independent Security Agent holds a first-priority, perfected security interest in the underlying assets for the benefit of tokenholders — meaning if something goes wrong, there's a legal structure empowering that agent to foreclose on the collateral and distribute proceeds back to holders. Dividends are automatically reinvested into the token's value (net of applicable withholding tax), so holders don't need to claim anything separately, and tokens are designed as total-return trackers of the underlying security.

Era Two: The Solana Wave

As Solana's DeFi ecosystem matured through faster block times and deeper on-chain liquidity, tokenized equities followed. xStocks brought public-company tokens onto Solana rails, giving Solana-native traders direct exposure to companies like Apple, Tesla, and Nvidia without ever leaving the ecosystem they already used for everything else.

PreStocks pushed the frontier even further. Rather than tokenizing public stocks, it tokenizes exposure to private, pre-IPO companies — names like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Anduril — through SPV-backed tokens. This is genuinely new access: a retail trader cannot buy shares of OpenAI on a normal brokerage account today at any price, at any size. PreStocks tokens give holders economic exposure only (no ownership, voting, dividend, or information rights, and no guaranteed secondary-market liquidity — this is a materially different risk profile from a fully-backed public-stock token), but the category itself proved something important: tokenized stocks aren't just a wrapper around something that already exists. They can unlock access that has no traditional equivalent at all.

Era Three: Robinhood Chain Takes The Spotlight

Robinhood Chain flips the underlying model. Instead of a DeFi protocol bolting stock exposure onto a general-purpose blockchain, this is a chain built specifically around tokenized real-world assets, with Robinhood Stock Tokens as the flagship product. Every Stock Token is a standard ERC-20 contract with 18 decimals, tied to a specific underlying equity or ETF and identified by its ticker. Prices are published on-chain through a live Chainlink data feed, so any smart contract can read the current price directly — no oracle integration work required. Because it's a standard ERC-20 with an oracle already wired in, developers can build lending markets, trading venues, and structured products on top of it with no special SDK — the same tools used for any ERC-20 just work.

Stock Tokens are self-custodied and accessible around the clock, but it's worth understanding the actual trading calendar underneath that claim. There are two separate windows to know:

The Tokenization Window

Market makers can mint and burn new Stock Tokens Monday 02:00 CET through Saturday 02:00 CET (subject to daylight savings). Outside that window, no new tokens are minted or burned.

On-Chain Buying & Selling

Once tokens exist, end users can buy and sell them on-chain at any time — including outside the tokenization window. This is the part that never stops.

Each token also has per-asset trading capabilities that vary by session — regular market hours, extended hours, and overnight — which developers can query through Robinhood's assets API before executing a trade. And because corporate actions like dividends and stock splits still happen to the underlying company, Robinhood Chain manages them through an on-chain multiplier (defined by ERC-8056, the Scaled UI Amount Extension) that adjusts the shares-per-token ratio automatically while your raw token balance stays static — the oracle folds the multiplier into the price it reports, so nothing needs to be manually claimed or reconciled.

Breaking · September 17, 2026

The Access Map Keeps Expanding

The whole thread running through this module is access: tokenized stocks have quietly turned a market that used to require a local broker, a funded account, and specific market hours into something anyone with a wallet can reach. Regulators around the world are now racing to build formal on-ramps for that reality instead of leaving it in a gray zone — and the most consequential move yet just landed in the United States.

What Just Happened

On September 17, 2026, the SEC issued Order 34-106402 — the "Innovation Exemption" — creating a new category called Tokenized Securities Venues (TSVs). A qualifying venue can now run permissioned automated-market-maker liquidity pools trading tokenized versions of real, listed U.S. stocks — the same National Market System (NMS) stocks that trade on Nasdaq and the NYSE — without registering as a full securities exchange. It's the clearest signal yet that one of the world's largest capital markets is building a formal, sanctioned bridge onto public blockchains rather than treating on-chain equity trading as something to be held at arm's length.

This didn't come out of nowhere. It's the next step after a string of moves through 2025 and 2026: a no-action letter letting the DTCC's Depository Trust Company begin tokenizing Russell 1000 stocks, major ETFs, and Treasuries; a joint SEC staff statement on tokenized securities; and rule changes clearing both Nasdaq and the NYSE to support tokenized share trading. It also lands alongside momentum elsewhere — the UK and South Korea both moving on their own tokenized-securities frameworks, and Ondo Stocks already operating under an EU-approved base prospectus that makes many of its tokens available to retail investors across the EEA. Taken together, the pattern is a global one: market infrastructure is being built out faster than any single regulator, and each new framework adds another region to the map rather than replacing the others.

What The Exemption Actually Requires

It's a real framework with real guardrails, not an open door. A TSV has to be a properly incorporated venue that meets a set of operating standards, and — importantly for investor protection — any tokenized stock traded on one has to precisely mirror the rights of the underlying security, including dividends and voting, where many existing tokenized-stock products (including several covered in this module) currently offer price exposure only. Purely synthetic look-alike products aren't permitted. And it's explicitly temporary: a five-year window the SEC is using to gather real data on how AMM-based securities trading performs before deciding what, if anything, becomes permanent.

Why This Matters For The Global Picture

The platforms covered in Module 1 already built a genuinely global access story without this: Ondo Stocks, PreStocks, and xStocks all serve investors across dozens of countries today. What this adds is depth rather than a replacement — one of the world's largest and most liquid equity markets now has a real, sanctioned path to plug into that same on-chain access layer, rather than sitting outside it. Expect the platforms named throughout this module, and new entrants, to move toward qualifying under frameworks like this one as they roll out region by region. The practical takeaway for anyone evaluating a platform, wherever you're based, is the same: check what's actually available to you today, since this is one of the fastest-moving access maps in finance right now.

Module 3

Why Supplying Liquidity Beats Just Holding

Picture two people who both believe Apple stock is going higher over the next year. Same conviction, same asset, same starting capital — but structurally different outcomes.

The Traditional Holder

Buys AAPL through a brokerage. If the price rises 12% over the year, they made 12%. If Apple pays a small dividend, they pick up a little more. That's the entire toolkit — price appreciation, plus whatever dividend the company decides to issue. The capital just sits there between now and the day they sell. It isn't doing anything else; it's simply exposed to the price.

The Liquidity Provider

Buys tokenized AAPL and pairs it into a liquidity pool (against a stablecoin or another asset). Now that same position is doing two jobs at once: it still carries the price exposure, and it earns a cut of every trade that swaps through that pool, 24 hours a day, every day the pool exists. The position that used to just sit there is now actively working — generating income independent of whether the price moves at all in a given hour.

The Trade-Off You're Accepting

This isn't free money — it's a different risk profile, and it deserves real explanation rather than a footnote. An LP position can experience impermanent loss: because a pool automatically rebalances its two assets as their relative prices move, if the paired assets drift apart in price, the combined value of your position can end up lower than if you had simply held the two assets separately instead of pooling them.

When impermanent loss bites hardest

Volatile pairs, sharp one-directional price moves, and wide price divergence between the two pooled assets over your holding window. A tokenized stock that suddenly gaps 20% on earnings news while paired against a stablecoin is the textbook case.

When it's minimal

Tighter, well-chosen price ranges, active rebalancing as the price moves, and pairs that tend to move together rather than apart. Blue-chip stock pairs during quiet, range-bound stretches see much smaller drag than volatile or thinly-traded pairs.

That's the real cost behind the fee income, and it's why range selection, pool choice, and active position management matter — this is the actual skill in stock-LP investing, not just depositing capital and walking away. Anyone teaching this strategy without covering impermanent loss in real depth isn't giving you the full picture, and you should be skeptical of any pitch that doesn't mention it.

The Weekend Edge

There are 168 hours in a week. The U.S. stock market is open for roughly 32.5 of them — 6.5 trading hours a day, five days a week. Everything else — nights, weekends, holidays — the traditional market is simply closed. Tokenized stock pools don't close.

What This Means Practically

A tokenized AAPL or NVDA pool can still be earning trading fees on Friday night, all weekend, and Monday morning before the opening bell — hours when a traditional brokerage account earns nothing beyond a static, unmoving price. Traders positioning in tokenized blue-chip pools ahead of a quiet weekend are specifically capturing that dead time, and doing it repeatedly, week after week, compounds into a real structural advantage over a buy-and-hold account that simply idles through the same hours.

In practice, this often means going a little tighter on your price range heading into a weekend (since the underlying isn't expected to move much while the exchange is closed) to maximize the share of fees you capture relative to other liquidity in the pool, then widening the range again once regular trading hours resume and volatility picks back up.

The Other Side Of It

Because the underlying stock isn't trading, price discovery over the weekend can be thinner and gaps can be more pronounced once TradFi markets reopen Monday. A position sized and ranged for a quiet weekend can be caught off guard by a Monday gap up or down — especially around earnings releases, major news, or macro events that land after Friday's close and get fully priced in only once the real exchange reopens. Range and position size should reflect that added uncertainty, not just the fee opportunity in isolation. The tighter you go for weekend fee capture, the faster a real Monday gap can push your position outside that range entirely.

Module 6

Turning Stock Tokens Into Liquid Capital

The other half of this strategy has nothing to do with trading fees at all — it's about not having to sell when you need cash. On-chain lending markets now accept tokenized stocks as collateral, letting you borrow stablecoins against a position you still fully own and still benefit from if it appreciates.

Why This Beats Traditional Margin

In traditional finance, unlocking cash from a stock position usually means either selling — triggering a taxable event where applicable and giving up all future upside on what you sold — or opening a margin loan through your broker, which comes with an application process, credit-based underwriting, and is often unavailable to most retail accounts at meaningful scale. On-chain, borrowing against a tokenized stock position is permissionless, transparent, and available to anyone holding the token: no application, no credit check, just collateral and a loan-to-value ratio enforced automatically by a smart contract.

The catch, stated plainly

These are still collateralized loans. If the stock token's price falls far enough, the position can be automatically liquidated to repay the loan — the same mechanical risk as any margin position, just enforced by code instead of a broker's margin desk. Automation makes it faster and more predictable, not risk-free.

Platform Spotlight: Ripe Finance

Robinhood Chain

Ripe is a multi-collateral borrowing protocol, originally deployed on Base and since extended to Robinhood Chain, where it directly accepts Robinhood Stock Tokens as collateral. Its core design choice is what separates it from older lending protocols like Aave or Compound: instead of one isolated vault per asset, Ripe lets you deposit your entire portfolio — crypto, yield-bearing positions, and tokenized stocks — into a single combined position, and borrow one stablecoin, GREEN, against the whole thing at weighted terms based on each asset's individual risk.

How The Loan Actually Works

GREEN targets a $1 peg and is burned whenever a loan is repaid, keeping supply tied to outstanding debt. A yield-bearing variant, sGREEN, auto-compounds protocol revenue for holders who'd rather stay passive rather than actively manage a loan. When you borrow, you're not selling or locking away your stock token's price exposure — it keeps moving with the market exactly as if you'd never touched it. If the combined value of your collateral falls far enough relative to what you've borrowed, the position becomes eligible for liquidation, where a liquidator repays part of your debt in exchange for seizing collateral at a discount — the same over-collateralization safety model used by established protocols like MakerDAO, just applied here to a basket that includes tokenized equities.

Worked example: deposit $10,000 of tokenized NVDA at 70% LTV, and you can borrow up to roughly $7,000 in GREEN — while your original $10,000 of NVDA exposure keeps moving with the market exactly as if you'd never touched it.

Worth Knowing Before You Deposit

Ripe is genuinely early-stage. Public on-chain trackers have shown its total value locked in the low single-digit millions or less — a fraction of established lending markets like Aave or Kamino. That's not disqualifying (every protocol starts small), but it does mean thinner liquidity, less battle-testing, and more sensitivity to a single large position than a deeply established market. Ripe also distributes a governance token, RIPE, through protocol vaults and community liquidity-mining farms — those farm APYs are typically driven by token emissions rather than real protocol revenue, and locked RIPE positions can carry a steep penalty (reported as high as 80%) for exiting early. Treat governance-token farming as a separate, higher-risk activity from the core collateralized-borrowing use case covered above.

Platform Spotlight: Kamino

Solana

Kamino Lend is the largest lending protocol on Solana, and in July 2025 it became the first major DeFi lending protocol anywhere to accept tokenized stocks as collateral — a genuine industry first, not just a feature update. It did this by building a dedicated xStocks Market on its v2 architecture, purpose-built to handle collateral that behaves differently from crypto: assets with real-world trading-hours schedules and equity-style volatility patterns.

Why The Architecture Matters

Kamino's xStocks Market runs as an isolated market, meaning risk from tokenized-stock collateral is walled off from Kamino's core crypto lending pools — a blowup or sharp depeg in one tokenized stock can't cascade into the rest of the protocol's $1B+ in overall deposits. Pricing runs through Chainlink Data Streams, a sub-second price feed built specifically for the xStocks integration, addressing the core structural challenge of lending against equities: the underlying stock market closes on weekends and holidays, but the DeFi protocol never does.

Platform Spotlight: Morpho — Coinbase Stock Tokens

Base

The newest entry in this module, and it's moving fast. Coinbase began issuing its own tokenized stocks in August 2026 — self-custodied, 1:1-backed tokens like AAPLc, NVDAc, GOOGLc, METAc, and SPCXc — and on September 7, 2026, the lending protocol Morpho opened live borrowing markets against five of them on Base. It's the same collateralized-borrowing idea as Ripe and Kamino, from a third major venue, and the activity so far shows just how quickly a brand-new market can go from empty to genuinely used.

How These Markets Are Built

Each market is independently curated — in this case, all five by Steakhouse Financial, whose USDC vaults supply the large majority of the lendable dollars. Chainlink price feeds value the underlying stock tokens for collateral calculations and liquidations, and borrowers can draw USDC either through Morpho's standard variable-rate markets or its "Midnight" fixed-rate layer, which lists a full ladder of maturities out to March 2027 for each token — though as of this writing, all real borrowing activity sits in the variable-rate side; the fixed-rate markets exist but haven't seen use yet.

Worth Knowing

Access to Coinbase's stock tokens themselves is currently limited to eligible investors outside a handful of restricted regions, and that eligibility is enforced at the token and issuer level rather than by Morpho's own market contracts — so whether you can actually acquire the underlying token to deposit as collateral is a separate question from whether the Morpho market exists. Five of Coinbase's ten listed stock tokens (Amazon, Microsoft, Strategy, SanDisk, and Tesla) don't have a Morpho market yet at all — check the live app for the current list rather than assuming a name from Module 1's roster is automatically borrowable here.

Module 7

The Flywheel

Put every piece together and you get a loop that compounds in a way a plain brokerage account structurally can't — because price appreciation, fee income, and liquidity access are all stacked on the same capital at once, and every layer feeds the next one.

Buy the stock token

Gain price exposure to a company you believe in — 24/7, self-custodied.

→

Supply it as liquidity

Earn ongoing fee income on top of price appreciation — including through nights and weekends.

→

Compound the fees

Reinvest earned fees back into the same or new stock positions instead of cashing out.

→

Borrow against the stack

Use Ripe or Kamino to unlock liquid capital without selling a single token.

→

Redeploy the capital

Open new positions, cover expenses, or DCA into more blue chips — the stack keeps growing underneath you.

Module 8

Risk, In Full

Every advantage covered above comes with a corresponding risk. None of this is a reason to avoid the strategy — it's the information you need to size positions and manage them responsibly.

Impermanent Loss

Pooled assets can drift apart in price, leaving your combined LP position lagging what simple holding would have returned. Covered in depth in Module 2 — this is the core trade-off behind every fee dollar earned.

Smart Contract Risk

Every protocol named in this module — Ondo, xStocks, PreStocks, Robinhood Chain's Stock Tokens, Coinbase's stock tokens, Ripe, Kamino, Morpho — is ultimately code. Bugs and exploits are a real, if uncommon, possibility on any DeFi protocol, no matter how established.

Liquidation Risk

Borrow against collateral and a sharp enough drawdown in the underlying stock token's price can trigger automated liquidation of your position — same mechanical risk as TradFi margin, enforced by a smart contract instead of a broker.

De-Peg / Issuer Risk

A tokenized stock is only as sound as the entity backing it and the collateral held behind it. Verification and security-agent structures (like Ondo's) reduce this risk but don't eliminate it entirely.

Regional Access Risk

Access rules differ by platform and by region, and they're evolving quickly right now — always verify what's actually available where you are before assuming access. New frameworks like the SEC's Innovation Exemption (Module 2) are also explicitly temporary experiments rather than permanent guarantees, so a venue's current access status is worth checking directly rather than assuming it holds indefinitely.

Weekend Gap Risk

Thinner weekend price discovery can mean sharper, less predictable moves once traditional markets reopen — covered in depth in Module 4.

Reference

Glossary

Tokenized Stock
An on-chain token designed to track the economic value of a real, publicly (or privately) held company's shares — price movement and often dividends — without granting the legal ownership rights a real share carries.
Liquidity Provider (LP)
Someone who deposits a pair of assets into a pool so others can trade between them, earning a share of the trading fees generated by that pool.
Impermanent Loss
The gap that can open up between holding two assets in an LP pool versus simply holding them separately, caused by the pool automatically rebalancing as prices move.
Loan-to-Value (LTV)
The percentage of your collateral's value you're allowed to borrow against — a 70% LTV on a $10,000 position means you can borrow up to $7,000.
Collateralized Borrowing
Depositing an asset (like a stock token) into a lending protocol to borrow another asset (usually a stablecoin) against it, without selling the original asset.
Just-in-Time Liquidity
A minting model (used by Ondo Stocks) that taps real exchange liquidity at the moment of a trade instead of relying on a pre-funded token pool — dramatically reducing price slippage.
Chainlink Price Feed / Oracle
An on-chain data source that reports a token's real-world price so smart contracts can read it directly, without needing to trust a centralized source.
ERC-8056 / Scaled UI Amount
The standard Robinhood Chain uses to reflect stock splits and dividends via an on-chain multiplier, so a holder's raw token balance stays static while their true value updates automatically.
Tokenized Securities Venue (TSV)
A venue category created by the SEC's September 2026 Innovation Exemption in the United States — a permissioned venue allowed to trade tokenized versions of real listed stocks via AMM liquidity pools without registering as a full securities exchange. One region's version of the access frameworks now emerging worldwide.
